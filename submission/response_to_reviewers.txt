Reviewer #1, Concern #1

Reviewer comment:

Reduced agreement is not shown to improve accuracy or any decision-relevant outcome. Link the
agreement-cost frontier to calibration, abstention, robustness, or tail-error reduction.

Author response:

  - We agree that reduced agreement alone does not establish a downstream benefit.
  - We added a selective-prediction analysis using mean agent conviction to rank ensemble
    decisions. AURC is 0.553 for HOM, 0.568 for HET-LITE, and 0.554 for HET. The HOM-HET
    difference is -0.001 with a 95% confidence interval of [-0.059, 0.060]. Under two-way equity
    and date resampling, the interval is [-0.113, 0.115]. Both intervals contain zero.
  - Restricting evaluation to the most confident half of decisions does not materially reduce
    selective risk in any configuration. We therefore do not claim an improvement in calibration
    or abstention quality.
  - We also evaluate a separate decision-relevant outcome, unanimous incorrect decisions. HOM
    produces unanimous incorrect directional decisions at 1.84 times the rate of HET. The equity-
    clustered 95% confidence interval is [1.42, 2.52], and the two-way interval is [1.13, 3.06].
    Because this analysis is post hoc and no multiplicity adjustment is applied, we treat it as
    exploratory rather than confirmatory.

Author action:

  - Section V-I, "Cross-Domain Replication: Clinical Knowledge," subsection "Selective
    Prediction," was added to report the risk-coverage analysis and uncertainty for the HOM-HET
    AURC contrast.
  - Section V-H, "Exploratory Analysis of Consensus Errors," was revised to report the unanimous-
    incorrect ratio with equity-clustered and two-way confidence intervals and to state explicitly
    that the analysis is exploratory.
  - Section V-D, "The Agreement-Cost Frontier," was revised to state that the frontier quantifies
    the cost associated with greater behavioral diversity and does not by itself establish
    improved downstream decision quality.

Reviewer #1, Concern #6

Reviewer comment:

Table 4 and the text disagree on costs. Clarify per-decision cost, token accounting, and pricing
assumptions.

Author response:

  - We clarified the cost calculation. For each model call, metered prompt and completion tokens
    are priced using the per-million-token rates reported in the model registry.
  - Costs are summed across the five agents within each ensemble decision and then averaged across
    cells within each configuration.
  - The resulting costs are $6.48 x 10^-4 for HOM, $1.52 x 10^-3 for HET-LITE, and $2.84 x 10^-3
    for HET. Moving from HOM to HET therefore increases inference cost by approximately 4.4x.
  - Prices are fixed at those applicable during data collection. No discount, batching, or caching
    adjustment is applied. Failed requests returning no billable completion are excluded.

Author action:

  - Section V-D, "The Agreement-Cost Frontier," was revised to state the token accounting,

Reviewer #2, Concern #2

Reviewer comment:

No clear explanation about fair comparison with current state-of-the-art approach.

Author response:

  - This article does not propose a new ensemble algorithm, so a direct task-performance
    competition with existing ensemble methods is not the primary comparison.
  - Existing methods such as mixture-of-agents, multi-agent debate, and diversity-based ensemble
    selection are generally evaluated through downstream performance. The present study instead
    measures how ensemble composition changes output agreement, error dependence, and inference
    cost under fixed task conditions.
  - The relevant comparison is therefore methodological. The study separates output agreement from
    error correlation, controls the shared-seed artifact observed in repeated model sampling,
    quantifies statistical uncertainty, and incorporates inference cost.

Author action:

  - Section V-L, "Relation to Existing Ensemble Methods," was added to explain the basis of
    comparison with existing ensemble approaches.
  - Section II-A, "LLM Ensembles and Mixture-of-Agents," provides the corresponding literature

Reviewer #3, Concern #3

Reviewer comment:

Fleiss' kappa measures agreement, not correlation. Either remove "de-correlation"/"less-correlated
judgments" throughout the title, abstract, figures, discussion, and conclusion, or add actual

agreement, error correlation, sampling independence, model diversity, and provider diversity.

Author response:

  - We took the second option and now measure error dependence directly.
  - For every pair of agents within an ensemble cell, we compute phi between binary error
    indicators. Error correlation decreases from 0.984 for HOM to 0.742 for HET-LITE and 0.530 for
    HET. The HOM-HET contrast is 0.454 with a 95% confidence interval of [0.376, 0.534]. Under
    two-way resampling, the interval is [0.328, 0.586].

    with HOLD counted as an error. The corresponding HOM-HET contrast is 0.309 with a 95%
    confidence interval of [0.262, 0.354]. The two-way interval is [0.227, 0.387].
  - Mean per-agent error rates remain similar across configurations, so the decline in phi
    reflects lower error dependence rather than differences in average correctness.
  - The manuscript now distinguishes output agreement, error correlation, sampling independence,
    model diversity, and provider diversity. Decorrelation is defined specifically as a reduction
    in error correlation measured by phi, while kappa measures categorical agreement.

Author action:

  - Section V-C, "Agreement, Error Correlation, and Decorrelation," was added to define the five
    concepts and report the phi analysis under both treatments of HOLD.
