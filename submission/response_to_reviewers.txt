Reviewer #1, Concern #1

Reviewer comment:

Reduced agreement is not shown to improve accuracy or any decision-relevant outcome. Link the
agreement-cost frontier to calibration, abstention, robustness, or tail-error reduction.

Author response:

  - We agree that reduced agreement alone does not establish a downstream benefit. We therefore
    added a selective-prediction analysis using mean agent conviction. AURC is 0.553 for HOM,
    0.568 for HET-LITE, and 0.554 for HET; the HOM-HET difference is -0.001 with a 95%
    equity-clustered interval of [-0.059, 0.060] and a two-way interval of [-0.113, 0.115].
  - Restricting evaluation to the most confident half of decisions does not materially reduce
    selective risk, so we do not claim improved calibration or abstention quality.
  - As a separate exploratory outcome, HOM produces unanimous incorrect directional decisions at
    1.84 times the rate of HET, with equity-clustered and two-way 95% intervals of [1.42, 2.52]
    and [1.13, 3.06], respectively. Because this analysis is post hoc and unadjusted for
    multiplicity, we treat it as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

Author action:

  - Section V-H, "Exploratory Analysis of Consensus Errors," was revised to report the
    unanimous-incorrect ratio with equity-clustered and two-way confidence intervals and to
    state explicitly that the analysis is exploratory.
  - Section V-D, "The Agreement-Cost Frontier," was revised to state that the frontier
    quantifies the cost associated with greater behavioral diversity and does not by itself
    establish improved downstream decision quality.
  - Section V-I, "Selective Prediction," is now a standalone subsection rather than part of the
    clinical replication, since its equity-clustered and two-way intervals concern the financial

Reviewer #2, Concern #2

Reviewer comment:

No clear explanation about fair comparison with current state-of-the-art approach.

Author response:

  - This study does not propose a new ensemble algorithm, so a leaderboard-style
    task-performance comparison with existing ensemble methods would address a different
    question. Prior work typically evaluates mixture-of-agents, debate, or ensemble-selection
    methods through downstream performance, whereas we hold the task, prompt, information set,
    and aggregation rule fixed and study how ensemble composition relates to agreement, error
    dependence, and cost.
  - The appropriate comparison is therefore methodological: our framework separates output
    agreement from error correlation, controls the shared-seed artifact, quantifies statistical
    uncertainty, and incorporates inference cost.

Author action:

  - Section V-M, "Relation to Existing Ensemble Methods," was added to explain the basis of
    comparison with existing ensemble approaches.
  - Section II-A, "LLM Ensembles and Mixture-of-Agents," provides the corresponding literature
    context for mixture-of-agents, diversity-based selection, and heterogeneous ensemble
    approaches.
  - Section V-M, "Relation to Existing Ensemble Methods," now states the distinction as one of
    estimand rather than quality: prior aggregation and selection work asks whether a method
    improves downstream performance, while this study holds the task, prompt, information set
    and aggregation rule fixed and asks how ensemble composition relates to agreement, error
    dependence and cost. It identifies the four methodological axes on which the comparison is
    appropriate, and cites Kuncheva and Whitaker [17] for the classifier-ensemble result that
    diversity and accuracy are related but not interchangeable, which is why reduced agreement
    is not treated here as evidence of improved accuracy.

Reviewer #3, Concern #3

Reviewer comment:

Fleiss' kappa measures agreement, not correlation. Either remove "de-correlation"/"less-correlated

Author response:

  - We took the reviewer's second option and now measure error dependence directly using φ
    correlations between paired binary error indicators. Error correlation decreases from 0.984
    for HOM to 0.742 for HET-LITE and 0.530 for HET, giving a HOM-HET contrast of 0.454 (95% CI
    [0.376, 0.534]; two-way [0.328, 0.586]).

    HOM-HET contrast of 0.309 (95% CI [0.262, 0.354]; two-way [0.227, 0.387]). Mean per-agent
    error rates remain similar, so the reduction reflects lower error dependence rather than
    differences in average correctness.
  - The manuscript now explicitly distinguishes output agreement, error correlation, sampling
    independence, model diversity, and provider diversity; "de-correlation" refers specifically
    to reduced error correlation measured by φ, while κ measures categorical agreement.

Author action:

  - Section V-C, "Agreement, Error Correlation, and Decorrelation," was added to define the five
    concepts and report the φ analysis under both treatments of HOLD.
  - Section V-A was retitled "Heterogeneity and Ensemble Agreement (Primary)" so that the κ
    result is named as an agreement result and the heading itself carries no causal verb.
  - The Abstract was revised to report the φ contrast alongside the κ contrast and to
    distinguish reduced error dependence from reduced agreement.
  - Section VI, "Discussion," and Section VIII, "Conclusion," were revised so that decorrelation
    claims refer to error dependence rather than categorical agreement. The Conclusion now
    states that model diversity can reduce both output agreement and error correlation.
  - Section V-C now states the phi computation exactly as implemented: within each
    (configuration, equity, date, run) cell we form, for every unordered pair of agent
    positions, the pair of binary error indicators against the realized forward-return sign;
    those pairs are pooled over all cells and all agent-position pairs within a configuration,
    and phi is the Pearson correlation of the pooled set, so one phi is reported per
    configuration rather than one per agent pair. The previous wording implied a correlation per
    pair, which a single paired observation cannot support.
  - Reference [17], Kuncheva and Whitaker on the relationship between ensemble diversity and
    accuracy, was added to the reference list to support the separation of reduced error
    dependence from improved accuracy.
