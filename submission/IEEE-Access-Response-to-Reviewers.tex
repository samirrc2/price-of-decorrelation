\concern{Reviewer \#1, Concern \#1}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{Reduced agreement is not shown to improve accuracy or any decision-relevant outcome. Link the agreement-cost frontier to calibration, abstention, robustness, or tail-error reduction.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item We agree that reduced agreement alone does not establish a downstream benefit.

\item We added a selective-prediction analysis using mean agent conviction to rank ensemble decisions. AURC is 0.553 for HOM, 0.568 for HET-LITE, and 0.554 for HET. The HOM-HET difference is -0.001 with a 95\% confidence interval of [-0.059, 0.060]. Under two-way equity and date resampling, the interval is [-0.113, 0.115]. Both intervals contain zero.

\item Restricting evaluation to the most confident half of decisions does not materially reduce selective risk in any configuration. We therefore do not claim an improvement in calibration or abstention quality.

\item We also evaluate a separate decision-relevant outcome, unanimous incorrect decisions. HOM produces unanimous incorrect directional decisions at 1.84 times the rate of HET. The equity-clustered 95\% confidence interval is [1.42, 2.52], and the two-way interval is [1.13, 3.06]. Because this analysis is post hoc and no multiplicity adjustment is applied, we treat it as exploratory rather than confirmatory.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Author action:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Section V-H, ``Exploratory Analysis of Consensus Errors,'' was revised to report the unanimous-incorrect ratio with equity-clustered and two-way confidence intervals and to state explicitly that the analysis is exploratory.

\item Section V-D, ``The Agreement-Cost Frontier,'' was revised to state that the frontier quantifies the cost associated with greater behavioral diversity and does not by itself establish improved downstream decision quality.

\item Section V-I, ``Selective Prediction,'' is now a standalone subsection rather than part of the clinical replication, since its equity-clustered and two-way intervals concern the financial experiment. \concern{Reviewer \#3, Concern \#2}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{Recompute uncertainty while accounting for both repeated observations within equities and shared analysis dates/common market conditions. Justify the bootstrap unit formally, evaluate two-way equity/date clustering or an appropriate multiway resampling strategy, and regenerate all main and exploratory confidence intervals accordingly.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Equity is the pre-registered primary resampling unit because the repeated observations in this design are nested within securities: each equity is evaluated on 12 analysis dates, and every response for that equity shares company-specific context. Analysis date is the second, crossed source of dependence, because all securities evaluated on a given date share common market conditions. Neither margin nests inside the other, so they are resampled independently rather than hierarchically.

\item The two-way procedure draws equities with replacement and dates with replacement, independently, retains the sampled equity-date intersections, and preserves multiplicity on both margins: a cluster drawn twice contributes twice. It uses 2,000 draws at the pre-registered seed and recomputes the full estimator inside every draw.

\item Primary HOM-HET agreement contrast, $\Delta$$\kappa$ = 0.3363: [0.3035, 0.3689] under equity clustering (100 clusters), [0.2989, 0.3656] under date clustering (12 clusters), [0.2786, 0.3877] under two-way resampling (1,200 intersections). All exclude zero.

\item The two pre-registered secondary agreement contrasts are measured on the same grid and are now reported under the same scheme. HOM-HET-LITE $\Delta$$\kappa$ = 0.249: equity [0.218, 0.280], two-way [0.208, 0.290]. HET-LITE-HET $\Delta$$\kappa$ = 0.087: equity [0.067, 0.105], two-way [0.045, 0.123]. Both continue to exclude zero.

\item The capability-matched control is measured on the same 100 equities and 12 dates and asserts that its interval excludes zero, so it receives the same treatment. $\Delta$$\kappa$(HOM-SameTier) = 0.315: equity [0.288, 0.341], two-way [0.270, 0.361]. Both exclude zero.

\item Every exploratory contrast that carries an inferential claim is also reported both ways. Error correlation $\Delta$$\phi$ = 0.454: equity [0.376, 0.534], two-way [0.328, 0.586]. The common-subset specification counting HOLD as an error, $\Delta$$\phi$ = 0.309: equity [0.262, 0.354], two-way [0.227, 0.387]. Unanimous-incorrect ratio 1.84: equity [1.42, 2.52], two-way [1.13, 3.06]. Pile-on $\Delta$ = 0.189: equity [0.160, 0.216], two-way [0.140, 0.232]. AURC $\Delta$ = -0.001: equity [-0.059, 0.060], two-way [-0.113, 0.115]. No conclusion changes under the wider scheme: the intervals that excluded the null still exclude it, and the AURC interval still contains zero.

\item Two groups of intervals are deliberately not given a two-way treatment, for reasons of design rather than convenience. The temperature-sensitivity analysis is a small supporting subgrid of eight equities and two analysis dates; two date clusters cannot support date-clustered or two-way inference, so its intervals remain clustered on equities only and the manuscript now states that this analysis contributes no independent multiway evidence. The clinical replication is indexed by MMLU items rather than by equities and dates, so the financial equity-date bootstrap does not apply to it; its intervals are clustered on items.

\item The Wilson intervals in the secondary directional-accuracy table are marginal and descriptive. They are retained as descriptive only and are not the basis of any inferential claim, so replacing them with a clustered interval would imply an inference the manuscript does not make.

\item One correction follows from this work rather than from a change of method. The implementation used for the robustness table and for the unanimous-incorrect ratio collapsed each draw to a set of equities, so an equity drawn twice counted once and each draw used only the distinct equities of a 100-draw multiset. That is subsampling, not a cluster bootstrap, and it understated the variance. Multiplicity is now preserved, which is why the equity-clustered robustness row reproduces the primary interval exactly and why the unanimous-incorrect interval is [1.42, 2.52] rather than the narrower [1.51, 2.35] previously reported. The resampling code now asserts multiplicity on both margins and fails if it is ever lost, so the error cannot recur silently.

\end{itemize}

\item The manuscript now distinguishes output agreement, error correlation, sampling independence, model diversity, and provider diversity. Decorrelation is defined specifically as a reduction in error correlation measured by $\phi$, while $\kappa$ measures categorical agreement.
