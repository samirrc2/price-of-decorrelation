\concern{Reviewer \#1, Concern \#1}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{Reduced agreement is not shown to improve accuracy or any decision-relevant outcome. Link the agreement-cost frontier to calibration, abstention, robustness, or tail-error reduction.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item We agree, and we have not claimed otherwise. The manuscript reports that heterogeneity produces no reliable gain in average directional accuracy; the reviewer is right that this leaves the frontier without a demonstrated downstream benefit.

\item We now test the most directly relevant of the four suggested outcomes, abstention, using a selective-prediction analysis, and we report the result even though it is null.

\item Ensemble decisions are ranked by mean agent conviction, coverage is swept from the most confident decision to the full set, and selective risk is the error rate among retained decisions. The area under the risk-coverage curve is 0.553 for HOM, 0.568 for HET-LITE and 0.554 for HET, against full-coverage risks of 0.562, 0.537 and 0.512. Restricting to the most confident half of decisions does not materially reduce risk in any configuration.

\item Conviction as elicited here does not rank errors usefully, so we claim no calibration or abstention benefit.

\item We do report one decision-relevant quantity that does move: the rate at which an ensemble is unanimous and wrong. See our response to Concern \#4.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Author action:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Added ``Selective prediction'' to the results, reporting the risk-coverage analysis and AURC for all three configurations.

\item The frontier is now stated explicitly as a description of what diversity costs, not as evidence that it improves decision quality.

\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}

\item We have not varied ensemble size or aggregation rule. Those are single-parameter sweeps on the frozen data rather than new evidence about generality, and we preferred to spend the revision on the domain question, which is the one that bears on whether the finding is a property of the task. We state that omission rather than leaving it unexplained.

\concern{Reviewer \#2, Concern \#2}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{No clear explanation about fair comparison with current state-of-the-art approach.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item The manuscript did not explain why it contains no head-to-head benchmark, and that was a gap.

\item This article does not propose an ensemble method. Methods such as mixture-of-agents, multi-agent debate and diversity-maximising ensemble selection are evaluated on task performance, and the comparison that matters for them is accuracy against a baseline. The object of study here is different: how much behavioral de-correlation a given ensemble composition buys and what it costs, holding task, prompt and information set fixed.

\item The fair comparison is therefore one of measurement. Prior work reporting ensemble diversity typically uses a single agreement index on pooled outputs, without separating agreement from error correlation, without an independent-draw protocol, and without interval estimates. The manuscript now shows that the first distinction changes the interpretation, the second changes the estimate roughly fourfold in our pilot, and the third determines whether a reported difference is resolvable at all.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Author action:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Added ``Relation to state-of-the-art ensemble methods''.

\end{itemize}

\concern{Reviewer \#3, Concern \#2}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{Recompute uncertainty while accounting for both repeated observations within equities and shared analysis dates/common market conditions. Justify the bootstrap unit formally, evaluate two-way equity/date clustering or an appropriate multiway resampling strategy, and regenerate all main and exploratory confidence intervals accordingly.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item We have done this, and the primary result survives it.

\item The equity is the pre-registered unit because it is the level at which repeated observations are nested: each security is evaluated on twelve dates and shares company-specific context. We now state that justification rather than assuming it.

\item Dates are a second, crossed source of dependence. We report the primary contrast under three schemes: equity clustering (100 clusters), date clustering (12 clusters), and a two-way scheme in which equities and dates are resampled independently.

\item The point estimate is 0.3363 in all three. The intervals are [0.3035, 0.3689] for equities, [0.2989, 0.3656] for dates and [0.2786, 0.3877] two-way. The two-way interval is the widest, as expected when two crossed dependence structures are admitted at once, and all three exclude zero.

\item The reviewer is right that the earlier equity interval, [0.3131, 0.3628], contradicted the primary [0.3035, 0.3689] reported in the abstract, Table 2, the Results and the Conclusion. They were presented as the same estimand and should have agreed exactly. They did not because the two were computed by different procedures: the primary bootstrap resamples equities with replacement and counts an equity drawn twice as two clusters, while the robustness table treated each draw as a set, so a repeated draw counted once and every draw used only the roughly 63 distinct equities of a 100-draw multiset. That is subsampling, not a cluster bootstrap, and it understated the variance. The robustness table now honours multiplicity and uses the pre-registered equity ordering, so its equity row reproduces the primary interval exactly; the code asserts that equality on every run and fails if it is ever broken. The date and two-way intervals widen for the same reason. No point estimate changed and every interval still excludes zero.

\item We note that the date margin has only twelve clusters, a regime in which cluster-robust inference is known to under-cover. The equity-clustered interval therefore remains the pre-registered primary and the other two are reported as sensitivity analyses.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Author action:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Added the bootstrap-unit justification to the uncertainty section.

\item Added a table reporting the primary contrast under all three resampling schemes.

\end{itemize}

\concern{Reviewer \#3, Concern \#3}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{Fleiss' kappa measures agreement, not correlation. Either remove ``de-correlation''/``less-correlated judgments'' throughout the title, abstract, figures, discussion, and conclusion, or add actual dependence/error-correlation analyses that justify those terms. Explicitly distinguish output agreement, error correlation, sampling independence, model diversity, and provider diversity.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item The reviewer is right that kappa measures agreement irrespective of correctness, and the original manuscript acknowledged the distinction without measuring the second quantity. We have taken the second option and added the analysis.

\item For every pair of agents within a cell we compute the Pearson correlation between their binary error indicators against the forward-return sign. Error correlation falls monotonically: 0.984 for HOM, 0.742 for HET-LITE and 0.530 for HET, giving 0.454 (95\% CI [0.376, 0.534]).

\item Because abstentions are excluded, the scored subset differs across configurations. We repeat the calculation counting HOLD as an error, which fixes an identical subset for every configuration: the contrast is then 0.309 (95\% CI [0.262, 0.354]). Both treatments give a monotone decline whose interval excludes zero, so the estimate is bracketed rather than dependent on how abstentions are handled.

\item Mean per-agent error rates are close across configurations, so the decline is a change in dependence and not a consequence of differing accuracy.

\item We also state that de-correlation is partial rather than complete: heterogeneous agents still err together more often than independence would imply.

\item We have added the five-way distinction the reviewer asks for, and we now define the term explicitly as sense (2), error correlation, with the phi contrast rather than the kappa contrast as its evidence.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Author action:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Added ``Agreement, error correlation, and what 'de-correlation' denotes'', containing the five-way distinction and the phi analysis under both label treatments.

\item The abstract now reports the phi contrast alongside the kappa contrast.

\end{itemize}
